The projection booth

What's actually happening when you hit "print my stub"

No magic, no black box you have to trust blindly — here's the reel, start to finish.

01

The training set

The model learned from 25,000 real movie reviews, split evenly between positive and negative, pulled from a public IMDB dataset. Nothing synthetic, nothing hand-picked.

02

Cleaning the text

Before anything is analyzed, review text is lowercased, stripped of HTML tags and URLs, cleared of punctuation and common filler words (stopwords), then reduced to word stems — so "loved," "loving," and "loves" all count the same.

03

Turning words into numbers

The cleaned text is converted into a TF-IDF vector — a weighting scheme that cares more about words that are distinctive to a review than words that show up everywhere.

04

The verdict

A logistic regression classifier, trained on those vectors, outputs a probability. Above 50% positive and the stub reads POSITIVE; otherwise, NEGATIVE. The confidence score is just that probability.

Where it falls short

It's a linear model reading word patterns, not a person reading for tone — sarcasm, mixed reviews, and heavy plot summaries with little opinion can trip it up. Treat the verdict as a fast first read, not a substitute for actually reading the review.
